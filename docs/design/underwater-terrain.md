# Underwater 3D terrain — implementation plan

Status: IN PROGRESS (2026-10-19, phases through H shipped on the branch; I planned 2026-10-20; revised the same day after an independent review against the code — its findings are folded in and marked "review:"). Public issue: NikaGeospatial/onlymap-js#50. Related: #26 (subsurface; its camera-under-a-basemap half stays deferred — this plan is the no-basemap case).

The goal this plan is judged by: **a colleague's Monterey Canyon seafloor dive, built on 0.4.3 with ten workarounds, rebuilt on the new version with none** — no `mapEl.core`, no `deck`, no `_`-prefixed name, `validate` clean — and shipped as the feature's example. The workaround → replacement table is the issue's; each phase below names the rows it closes.

## Measured baseline (0.13.1)

- Negative elevations draw. `DECODERS.terrarium.offset = -32768` (`src/terrain.ts:46`) passes them through; `terrain="terrarium"` over Monterey Bay draws the canyon basin at z < 0, 3× exaggeration, no deck errors. A height-field read at the deep point returns −1,812 m.
- The free DEM has no seabed above zoom 10. AWS Terrarium carries ETOPO1 bathymetry (~1.8 km cells) at z ≤ 10 only; from z ≥ 11 the ocean decodes to 0 m (−629 m at z10, 0 at z12 for one point). Not documented anywhere.
- The camera already goes under. `standaloneView()` builds `new MapView({ repeat: true, farZMultiplier: 10 })` (`src/runtime-core.ts:1022`, since 0.6.2). With deck's view-state `position` set to `[0, 0, −6000]` the eye sat at −1,545 m with the frame fully drawn. The 0.4.3 page's +8,000 m scene lift is obsolete **for the camera** — see Spike 0 for the other reason it may have existed.
- `max-pitch` exists (0.8.1; `applyMaxPitch`, `src/elements/om-map.ts:1803`; standalone controller override at `src/runtime-core.ts:3163`, basemap adapter at 3186 → `src/basemap.ts:533`). The 0.4.3 page's `maxPitch: 85` through `core.deck` is obsolete.
- `terrain=` is one DEM: `parseTerrainAttrs` → one `TerrainIR` → one `buildTerrainLayer` (`src/terrain.ts:179, 353`), `operation: "terrain+draw"`, `pickable: "3d"` (a real deck mode: `lib/deck.js:557` `_shouldUnproject3D`), no `extent`. `TerrainIR` and `parseTerrainAttrs` are PUBLIC exports (`src/index.ts:100, 106`).
- Review: the view-state `position` ALREADY round-trips. `toDeckViewState` spreads the whole state (`src/runtime-core.ts:1002` `return { ...vs, ...extra }`), `fromDeckViewState` only strips four transition keys (251-256), and deck's `onViewStateChange` hands back the controller's full viewport props (`position`, `maxZoom`, `width`, `height`, …; `map-controller.js:79-96`). So after the first gesture the runtime's copy carries `position: [0,0,0]` and every `{ ...getViewState() }` spread leaks it: `src/programmatic.ts:1009` (`CameraState`), `src/elements/om-map.ts:1835` (feeds `om-view-changed`, 290), `buildViewportSnapshot` (`programmatic.ts:775`), `warm-tiles` (`actions.ts:1302`). Only the TS type (`ViewState`, 213) is missing. Any non-zero `position.z` this plan introduces leaks the same way unless those sites pick fields.
- The standalone controller is deck's stock `MapController` (`standaloneController`, 246). deck 9.3.5 also ships `TerrainController extends MapController` (`@deck.gl/core`, unused here). Review: it is narrower than its name — it depth-picks the viewport centre every 500 ms through `pickPosition` (sync picking only; null on WebGPU's async path), rebases `position` ONCE ("on first successful pick", `terrain-controller.js:32`) and afterwards blends the altitude only inside `updateViewport`, which gestures call and transitions bypass (`controller.js:241`). The rebase changes `zoom` (`_rebaseViewport`, 108-122: a 2 km eye over a −1.8 km seabed drops zoom ~0.9 with no visual change), so tile selection, `visible-zoom-range` gates, cluster expansion and every saved `zoom` shift. Its `getAltitude` prop is the ROTATION pivot only and is overwritten by `MapController.setProps` (`map-controller.js:469`) — not a terrain-following input.
- Review — the decisive one: **deck's terrain height map clamps negative heights to sea level.** `terrain-pass.js:42` clears the map to `[0,0,0,0]`, blends with `blendColorOperation: 'max'` (6), and the write is `fragColor = vec4(commonPos.z, …)` (`shader-module.js:67`). For a seabed `commonPos.z < 0`, `max(0, z) = 0`: every layer draped in `offset` mode (the default for 3D models, point clouds, popups — `defaultTerrainMode`, `src/terrain.ts:286` — and for any layer with `instancePositions`/`extruded`) sits at z = 0 above the canyon. `drape`-mode layers are unaffected (they render into per-tile terrain covers sampled by the mesh, `terrain-effect.js:159`). Also: the fragment returns from `fs:#main-start` in the height-map pass BEFORE any `DECKGL_FILTER_COLOR` hook runs, and with two terrain sources `max` picks the HIGHER surface in an overlap — which is why the 0.4.3 page pushed its context tiles 200 m DOWN.
- Multiple `operation: "terrain"` layers are supported by `TerrainExtension` (`terrain-effect.js:53` filters all of them; one cover per terrain tile). Cost: every draped layer renders once per terrain tile of EVERY source, and the picking pass re-renders every cover (`terrain-effect.js:172`).
- `getCamera()` is promised in README.md:395, the skill (`references/syntax.md:53`), `docs/cartography.md`, `docs/design/carto-substrate.md`, `docs/design/globe.md` and `src/plane-camera.ts:6`, but exists nowhere (`MapController` has `getViewState(): CameraState`, five fields).
- Terrain presets carry no attribution (`TerrainPreset`, `src/terrain.ts:50-66`); `RuntimeCore.attributions()` (3001) credits basemaps only and the sheet is told through `onAttributionsChange` (3009), a push.
- No terrain e2e spec; the live terrain check is `e2e/examples.spec.ts:1473` (@slow, network). `e2e/dem-fixture.ts` serves ONE synthetic Terrarium field at `**/dem/*/*/*.png` (`serveDem`, 105) whose `heightAt` already reaches ≈ −3,600 m, so an underwater e2e needs no new encoder, only a second field/route.
- `center`/`zoom` attributes are initial-only; a mounted map moves through `setViewState`/`flyTo`. (A probe that forgets this measures Yosemite while believing it is over Monterey — it happened once while writing this.)

## Phases

Order: **Spike 0, 0, C, B, D, E, A, F and G done; H partly; I planned.** Spike 0 decides whether a deck patch is needed before anything else is built on the terrain pass. C is cheapest and closes the most rows; B has the second spike; A waits on a data-source decision; E is the proof.

### Spike 0 — Negative heights in the terrain height map (1 day, first)

The 0.4.3 page's +8,000 m lift is obsolete for the camera but NOT shown obsolete for offset-mode layers. The dive example has a gauge and a credit line only, so it could ship without one — but a seafloor map with a marker, a model or a point cloud is the normal case, and the library must not place it at sea level.

- Reproduce: `terrain="terrarium"` over the canyon + a `ScatterplotLayer` (offset by default) at a −1,800 m point; read its drawn z.
- Fix candidates, in order of preference:
  1. **Patch the terrain pass** — the repo already patches five deck files for projection (`dev/tools/gpu-projection/gen-deck-patch.mjs`, byte-guarded by `src/plane-gpu/deck-patch.test.ts`). Clear the height map to a large negative instead of 0 (the texture must be a float format for that — check `terrain-effect.js`'s height-map texture; if it is, `clearColor: [-1e5, 0, 0, 0]` and `max` then picks the real height, negative included). One line in `terrain-pass.js`, generated and guarded like the others.
  2. A library-side datum shift (`terrain-datum`) — the 0.4.3 lift done inside the runtime: every decoder offset raised, every readout, pick, measure and clip-box lowered back. Touches far more code and is the wrong altitude for the problem. Only if the texture is not float.
- Output: a decision recorded here, and an e2e spec on Metal and SwiftShader.

**Decision (2026-10-19) — IMPLEMENTED, two deck files (the sixth and seventh patches).** Reproduced first: an `offset` dot over a flat −2,000 m seabed drew at y = 299.5 px, sea level (300.0), not the ground (322.4); `drape` and a +2,000 m plateau were right — exactly the predicted failure. The fix moves the height map's CLEAR below any real ground (`terrain/terrain-pass.js`: −16 common units, ~1,250 km at the equator) and leaves deck's height write exact; the read (`terrain/shader-module.js`) takes `r` where alpha is 1 (any covered texel), rebuilds a coverage edge from `r = a·z + (1 − a)·CLEAR` (the map is LINEARLY filtered — `createRenderTarget` without `interpolate: false` — so a plain sentinel would bleed there), and reads sea level where nothing wrote (a = 0, deck's 1×1 dummy too). The other `terrain_map` read (drape covers, `fs:DECKGL_FILTER_COLOR`) is untouched.
- First version, replaced the same day: a datum ADDED to every height on write (+16) and subtracted on read. It lifted every height into float32's coarse range — ~0.15 m resolution — and in Phase C's close-range tests a marker sitting exactly on a +2,000 m plateau sank below it at 51 m clearance on Metal (the clear-based fix shows it). Lesson: never shift data into a coarser float range to dodge a clear value; patch the clear.
- `dev/tools/gpu-projection/gen-deck-patch.mjs` generates `src/plane-gpu/deck-patch/terrain-pass.js` and `terrain-shader-module.js` (renamed — deck has several `shader-module.js`); `dev/vite-plugin-deck-plane.mjs` maps both; `src/plane-gpu/deck-patch.test.ts` asserts seven byte-guarded files. Both shipped bundles carry them.
- `e2e/gpu-underwater.spec.ts` + `e2e/fixtures/underwater.html` + `serveConstantDem` (`e2e/dem-fixture.ts`): offset and drape points over a −2,000 m seabed and a +2,000 m plateau, and a 3× seabed, on Metal and SwiftShader.
- Known, not ours: from ~50 m away SwiftShader's depth test hides an OFFSET marker sitting exactly on the ground behind that ground — on land as at sea, with exact heights. Draped markers are unaffected.
- Consequence for the rest of the plan: the 0.4.3 page's +8,000 m lift is obsolete for markers too; no datum attribute. Phase B's spike now relies on this (one writer per texel, read back exactly).

### Phase C — Terrain-following camera, ground clearance, `getCamera()` (4–5 days)

Closes: "aim the camera at the seafloor every frame", "refuse zoom-in under 15 m", "hand-written tile sampler for the gauge"; the obsolete lift and `maxPitch` rows need only the doc line.

**C1. `camera-target="terrain"` — IMPLEMENTED (2026-10-19), a runtime-side follow.**
- `RuntimeCore.setCameraTarget` / `<om-map camera-target="sea-level|terrain">`. deck's frame lift (the view state's `position` z) follows the ground under the view centre (`GroundCache`, ×exaggeration); `zoom` keeps meaning scale at the target. Gestures EASE it (0.15 per frame, snap within 0.5 m), on their own frames in `onViewStateChange` and on animation frames after they stop; instant programmatic moves (`setViewState`, `zoomBy`) and anything under the determinism switch SNAP it, so a `seek()` frame is a function of the view; a transition (flight, inertia) HOLDS it — `FlyToInterpolator` compares `position`, so editing it mid-flight would cancel the flight — and deck's `onInteractionStateChange` resumes the follow when it ends. `whenSettled` waits for the lift to reach the ground (a failed tile cannot hold it: `GroundCache.isLoading`). Follow frames carry the origin of the move that caused them. Terrain off, `sea-level`, or a remount (getViewState is five fields) drops the lift to 0.
- **Found by the gesture e2e: deck's pan and wheel zoom grabbed the z = 0 plane.** With the eye below sea level the pointer's ray met that plane behind the camera — a 150 px drag moved the view 590 m the WRONG way. New `src/ground-controller.ts` `GroundController extends MapController`: it wraps deck's (unexported) `MapState` from `this.ControllerState`, unprojects the pointer at the lift's own height (`targetZ`), and moves with deck's `panByPosition3D` for pan and zoom. Used whenever a terrain is followed or kept clear of; the double-click zoom unprojects on the same plane. The per-controller link to the runtime is a `WeakMap`, not a class field (deck's constructor may call `setProps` before subclass fields exist).
- Validation: unknown value error; with `crs` error; `terrain` with no terrain warning. html-data entry.
- `TerrainController` (deck's own) stays rejected: zoom rebase, gesture-only follow.

**C2. `camera-clearance="<metres>"` — IMPLEMENTED (2026-10-19).**
- `maxZoomForClearance` (`src/camera-altitude.ts`): the eye's height above the frame lift scales exactly as 2^−zoom, so the highest zoom keeping the eye `clearance` true metres above the ground beneath it is closed-form; re-taken every frame as the eye moves over different ground. `GroundState.applyConstraints` caps zoom with it — deck runs that on every gesture state and every transition frame, so wheel, pinch, drag over a ridge and flights all hold; `clearOfGround` applies the same bound to instant moves, `zoomBy` and follow frames.
- Revised from the plan: the cap is on ZOOM only (a tilt or a pan over higher ground pulls the eye back rather than being refused), which is the "never through the ground" promise without a pitch-specific constraint. The reviewer's spring-back concern applies only to a cap that moves under a still camera; here the cap moves only when the eye's ground does.
- Validation: not a non-negative number error; no terrain warning. html-data entry.
- Measured: a wheel zoom from 13 stops at 19.42 with the eye at −1,950 m over a −2,000 m seabed (clearance 50); the drag then pans 15 m the right way.

**C3. `getCamera()` — IMPLEMENTED (2026-10-19).**
- `getCamera()` on `<om-map>`, `MapController` and `RuntimeCore`: `{ longitude, latitude, zoom, pitch, bearing, altitude, targetElevation, clearance }` — flattened from the first draft's `target: { elevation }`. `altitude` the eye against sea level, `targetElevation` the ground under the view centre, `clearance` the eye above the ground beneath it; TRUE metres (scene ÷ exaggeration). `altitude` null on a projected map; the ground fields null without terrain and until the tile lands. Exported type `Camera`.
- `src/camera-altitude.ts` (`eyeOf`, `trueMetres`): deck's own `WebMercatorViewport` with the frame lift, `unprojectPosition(cameraPosition)`. A tilted eye moves 0.1 % more than the lift (deck scales `position` at the centre's latitude; the eye sits km south) — deck's drawing, so kept.
- `src/terrain-ground.ts` (`GroundCache`): synchronous ground from the DEM tiles already asked about, a background fetch (`fetchHeightTile`, now exported from the height field) for a missing one, `null` meanwhile, a retry after 30 s for a failed tile, 32 tiles kept; a tile landing fires `onViewportChange("programmatic")` so a settled reader updates. One per terrain, dropped on change and destroy.
- `om-view-changed` fires on SETTLE (debounced), so its detail now carries the same three fields but a live gauge polls `getCamera()` from `requestAnimationFrame` — public API, not a workaround; the proof page does that.
- Leak fix, at the source: `RuntimeCore.getViewState()` returns the five authored fields (deck's `width`/`height`/`maxZoom`/`position`/… had leaked into `MapController.getViewState()`, the React adapter, snapshots and the remount since deck's controller hands them back). The remount therefore resets `position`, as C1 needs.
- Tests: `camera-altitude.test.ts` (top-down 1.5 heights, cos(pitch), the lift), `terrain-ground.test.ts` (null → height → arrival, one fetch, retry interval, dispose), `runtime-camera.test.ts` (five keys after a full deck viewport; the eye follows the lift), `gpu-underwater.spec.ts` (a 3× seabed at −2,000 m reads −2,000; clearance − altitude = 2,000). Unit suite 2,401/2,401.

### Phase B — Multi-source terrain — IMPLEMENTED (2026-10-19)

Closes: "two TerrainLayers with a 200 m push baked into the tiles", "textures shaded from un-pushed heights to hide the join".

**Grammar, as planned.** `<om-terrain>` children ranked in `terrain="survey, context"` (`parseTerrainSources`, src/terrain.ts): each child's `src`/`decoder`/`max-zoom`/`texture` resolve through the single-source `parseTerrainAttrs` (presets, keys and decoder rules unchanged), plus `extent`, `min-zoom`, `mesh-max-error`; the map keeps `terrain-exaggeration`, and a source without `texture` takes the map's `terrain-texture`. A plain preset or URL is exactly as before. At most 4 sources (the shader carries three higher-ranked rects). The element re-resolves when its `<om-terrain>` markup changes (`reconcileTerrainSources`, the `<om-effect>` pattern).

**IR — additive, not the reshape the first draft planned.** `TerrainIR` keeps its fields as the FIRST source's and gains an optional `sources` list (`terrainSources(ir)` reads either); every existing caller and the public `TerrainIR` type keep working.

**Mechanism.** `buildTerrainLayers`: one `TerrainLayer` per source (`__onlymapjs-terrain/<id>`; a single source keeps `__onlymapjs-terrain`), `extent` passed through, and `TerrainPriorityExtension` (src/terrain-priority.ts): a shader MODULE (deck merges extension `inject` strings, losing the order) whose `fs:#main-start` discard at order −1 runs before deck's terrain module writes the height map and returns. Each source keeps only its own extent AND drops every higher-ranked one — `extent` alone only limits which tiles LOAD, and an edge tile overhangs it, which would z-fight in a ring. The spike's one surprise: TerrainLayer's tile meshes are `COORDINATE_SYSTEM.CARTESIAN` in Web Mercator world units, so the extension converts each corner with `lngLatToWorld` before the layer's own `projectPosition` (the first run discarded every fragment). Measured: inside a survey extent 19,044 survey-texture pixels and 0 context, outside the reverse; an offset point stands on −1,000 m inside and −3,000 m outside, on Metal and SwiftShader.

**CPU readers take the same ranking.** `loadHeightfield` (profiles): each point reads the first-ranked source whose extent holds it, falling down only where that source has no data. `loadHeightfieldForBounds` (the volume grid, which integrates ONE regular grid): the first source holding the whole box, else the last. `GroundCache` (the camera): by rank, and while the first-ranked tile is loading it answers null rather than borrowing the next source's height (no jump between datasets).

**Validation.** The live parse's errors (unknown name, bad extent, a source with no src, more than 4); a non-last source with no extent is an error (it hides everything below); an `<om-terrain>` the map does not name, and map-level `terrain-decoder`/`terrain-max-zoom` beside sources, are warnings. html-data: the `om-terrain` element.

**Not done:** the join between sources is a true step if the datasets disagree there (no blending); drape cost doubles inside an overlap (every draped layer renders into each source's tile covers) — unmeasured on a real dataset, measure on the dive example. The `set-terrain` action's value is still one string; it can name a ranking, never edits children.

**Tests:** `src/terrain-sources.test.ts` (parse, errors, layers and their partition props, height field and volume source by rank, the camera waiting for the survey, validation); `e2e/gpu-underwater.spec.ts` two-source cases with flat-colour textures per source (`serveSolidTiles`), Metal and SwiftShader.

### Phase D — Sea surface and slope tint — IMPLEMENTED (2026-10-19)

Closes: "sand vs rock by slope painted into the texture tiles".

**`terrain-tint="slope"`** (`src/terrain-tint.ts`, option (a) as planned): `TerrainTintExtension` — a shader module (not extension `inject`, which loses order) at `fs:DECKGL_FILTER_COLOR`, order −1, AHEAD of deck's terrain module, which composites draped layers there and returns. Slope from screen-space derivatives of the mesh's common position (isotropic), true slope = atan(horizontal, vertical × exaggeration), blended `terrain-tint-flat` → `terrain-tint-steep` (defaults #d8c9a3 sediment, #7d7266 rock) over 15°–35°; it MULTIPLIES the lit colour, so shading survives and a texture is tinted. Faceted per triangle, like the mesh. `TerrainIR.tint`, map-level for every source. Measured with inclines of one known slope (`serveSlopeDem`): 8° all flat-coloured at ×1 and at ×3 (drawn 23°), 40° all rock at both. A depth gradient (the colleague's pale shelf → dark deep) followed as `terrain-tint="depth"`, Phase G.

**`sea-surface="<colour>"`** (`src/sea-surface.ts`): a translucent plane at z = 0, last in the stack, `depthWriteEnabled: false` — land above sea level hides it (depth test), the seabed shows through. Revised from a world quad to a `TileLayer` of per-tile quads (`refinementStrategy: "no-overlap"`): a polygon reaching 180° from a zoom-19 view has vertices so far off screen that rasterisation loses precision. No alpha given → ~35 %. Standalone only. Measured: over a 10° incline crossing sea level, 50 % blue below (85, 85, 213 from 170 grey) and the land above untouched.

Both validated (unknown tint error; a tint colour without the tint, warning; not-a-colour error — decidable only where it can be: a `#` value anywhere, a named colour in a browser, via new `isCssColor`, so the Node validate CLI never false-errors on `tan`; sea-surface on `crs` error, over a flat basemap warning) and in html-data.

### Phase E — The proof: rebuild the dive — PROVEN LOCALLY (2026-10-19); shipping it awaits a hosting decision

The colleague's page rebuilt on this branch (kept OUT of the repo — scratchpad, served by a Playwright route with his own tiles): `<om-map basemap="none" terrain="multibeam, regional" terrain-exaggeration="2" camera-target="terrain" camera-clearance="15" max-pitch="85" validate>`, two `<om-terrain>` sources (his NOAA multibeam z8–14 over his regional grid z5–11, his textures), and a gauge that is a `requestAnimationFrame` loop over `map.getCamera()` — about 15 lines of script against his ~110. Measured on Metal:

### Phase G — Depth tint, and the sea surface as a switch — IMPLEMENTED (2026-10-20)

Asked for after F: colour the seabed by depth, and let the dive's viewer turn the blue plane off.

**`terrain-tint` takes tokens:** `"slope"`, `"depth"`, or both (`"slope depth"`, any order, space or comma separated). An unknown token is ignored, and validation reports it as an error that says whether anything is still drawn. `"none"` alongside a tint is an error too. `terrainTintTokens` (src/terrain.ts) is the one tokenizer, shared by the live parse and validation, so the two can't disagree. `TerrainIR.tint` was reshaped to `{ slope?: { flat, steep }, depth?: { shallow, deep, maxDepth } }`. This is safe because the tint has not been published (0.13.1 predates Phase D).

**Depth** (in `TerrainTintExtension`, the same module as slope and the same hook, slope first): the true depth is `-geometry.worldPosition.z / exaggeration`. Below sea level, `color = mix(color × shallow, deep, sqrt(clamp(depth / maxDepth)))`. The ground is tinted by the shallow colour at the coast, and the deep colour takes over with depth, entirely at `terrain-tint-max-depth` (default 3,000 m). Land is untouched, so the coastline is where the colour starts.

The first version MULTIPLIED a linear shallow → deep ramp, and it failed on the real data in two ways. The view's seabed spans 0–1,000 m, so a linear ramp to 3,000 m left shelf and canyon floor alike. And a pale aqua multiplied into the sediment yellow gave olive, not water. The blend reads as water absorbing the ground's colour. The square root suits seabeds, which are mostly shelf: at 100 m it is already a fifth of the way to deep. Both act before lighting, so relief stays shaded at any depth. Defaults: shallow #c4e2e8, deep #26426e. Attributes `terrain-tint-shallow`, `terrain-tint-deep`, `terrain-tint-max-depth`. Each warns without its kind; a bad colour or a non-positive depth is an error. html-data entries.

**The example:** `terrain-tint="slope depth"`, and a "Sea surface" checkbox on the gauge that removes the `sea-surface` attribute and puts it back. That is the whole toggle, and it touches no runtime internals, as the example gate requires. Without the plane the canyon still reads as under water: it turns blue as it deepens and the land keeps its imagery. The gauge, crosshair and credit were `position: fixed`, measured from the window. The dev shell makes room for its header and sidebar by padding `<body>`, so they sat under the sidebar, the crosshair was off the view centre the gauge reads, and `height: 100vh` overflowed by the header. They now sit in a `position: relative` stage around the map (`height: 100%`), so they follow the map's box wherever a page frames it. Measured with the sidebar open and closed: the crosshair is 0 px from the map's centre, the checkbox can be clicked, and the page doesn't scroll. Three other examples use `position: fixed` overlays (google-3d-tiles, google-3d-flyby, shift-basemaps) and were not changed here.

**Tests:** `src/terrain-tint.test.ts` (tokens, defaults, overrides, the layer's switches and exaggeration) and `src/validation.test.ts` (tokens, each colour's kind, max-depth). In `e2e/gpu-underwater.spec.ts`, on the 10° incline with shallow red and deep blue to 2,000 m: red at the coast (~20 m), blue by the left edge (~2,600 m), the middle between them, the land with no tinted pixel. At ×3 exaggeration the middle keeps its hue to within 0.08 (a drawn-depth tint would move it about 0.2). Passes on Metal and SwiftShader.

### Phase H — Cracks between terrain tiles — IMPLEMENTED (2026-10-20), partly

**Then the seam Adam saw (2026-10-20), from 45 km up: the survey's extent as straight lines, and blue patches inland.** Four causes, found one at a time:
1. *The partition's step was see-through.* `TerrainBackfillExtension` (terrain-priority.ts): each lower-ranked source gets a backfill copy (`…/backfill`, a plain drawn layer, its tiles limited to the box around the extents above it). It draws only under the higher-ranked extents, with its depth squeezed into [0.999, 1), so every real surface wins and it shows only where nothing drew. A continuous sheet, it crosses every ray through the step. The first version made the real context layer write `gl_FragDepth` everywhere instead, which dropped deck's per-layer polygon offset, and an offset marker just behind a raised survey's far edge vanished (the existing GPU test caught it). Hence the separate layer: a shader that writes gl_FragDepth on one path must write it on all. deck's `terrain` uniform is not in every variant of the mesh shader, so pass decisions are made in JS from `shaderModuleProps`.
2. *Blue haze on low land.* deck pulls each later layer toward the camera by polygon offset (−100 × layerIndex). The sea plane is last, so from tens of km up it won the depth test over coastal land a few metres above sea level. Fixed with `getPolygonOffset: () => [0, 0]` on the sea surface.

The two now agree to a metre at the edge (they disagreed by a median of 19 m). Both push checks fail the script if either push survives.
4. *Backfill order.* Drawn before the sea, the far-plane backfill counted as behind the translucent plane, which tinted its slivers blue even on land. Drawn after it (runtime-core puts `…/backfill` layers last), it lost the tint underwater and showed as a grey line on the seabed (Adam's screenshot). The backfill now applies the sea's blend itself, where the eye and the ground are on opposite sides of sea level (`cameraAbove` from the frame's camera, the ground's sign from `geometry.worldPosition.z`). It is consistent from above and from below.

Measured in the dev shell: no line along the extent with styling off, nor with imagery, tint and sea on, from 45 km up and close to the edge, on land and over deep water. GPU test: two sources a 2 km step apart, the strip under the survey's near edge is 0 background pixels (2,160 without the backfill) and, under a sea surface, fully sea-tinted (0 without the backfill's own tint). Both assertions were checked to fail without their fix.

The same limit hits `terrarium` capped at z10 for its seabed. ### Phase I — Your own survey, looking right — IMPLEMENTED (2026-10-20), all five pieces, spec reviewed and amended

Asked after H: what it takes a user with "normal" data (their own survey plus a public grid) to get a map with no overt holes and at most moderate stitched verticals. Today the presets look right with one attribute, and two sources no longer leak the background where they meet. But a user's own tiles still hit four things the library has no answer for, in the order they meet them: tiling a GeoTIFF at all; no-data cells drawn as pits; a rectangular `extent` for an irregular footprint, whose edge triangles dip toward whatever lies outside; and a vertical-datum offset between datasets, doubled by exaggeration, which is the usual stitched vertical. Phase H's trench was the extent problem with a 200 m push standing in for no-data; a real survey's filler (0, −9999, −32768) does the same.

Five pieces, each shippable alone. Order: **1, 2, 3 first (holes gone, verticals explained); 4 then 5.**

**1. No-data on a source** — `<om-terrain nodata="-32768">` (also `nodata` on the map for a single DEM). A height equal to the value (after decoding, ± 0.5 m for Terrarium's rounding) is MISSING, not ground.
- *Mesh.* `SkirtedTerrainLayer.loadTerrain` already re-implements deck's; after the loader returns, drop every triangle with a vertex at the no-data height (indices rewritten; POSITION untouched; the bounding box recomputed from the kept vertices, since one sentinel vertex would otherwise sink zRange by 30 km). No shader test: a discarded fragment on an interpolated height would leave a ragged half-triangle. The worker loader is kept (the image decode and tesselation stay off the main thread); only the index pass runs here.
- *Where the hole shows the next source.* Colour pass: the backfill (Phase H) already draws every lower-ranked source behind everything, so a hole shows it with no new work. Height map (what `offset` layers stand on): the lower-ranked source's write is discarded under the upper's extent (terrain-priority.ts), so a marker in a hole would stand at sea level. Decision needed: **(a)** let lower-ranked sources write the height map everywhere — the pass max-blends, so a marker stands on the HIGHER source where both have data, not the ranked one (wrong where a survey sits below a coarse grid, ~half the time); **(b)** render the height map in rank order, lowest first, each source's write REPLACING (not max-blending) where it draws — one line in the seventh deck patch (`terrain-pass.js` blend) plus ordering the terrain layers by reverse rank for that pass only; **(c)** leave offset layers at sea level in holes. Recommend (b): it also makes the height map exact under overlaps for free. The CPU readers (`loadHeightfield`, `GroundCache`) treat the value as "no data here", falling to the next source, which they already do for a missing tile.
- *Extent becomes optional for the survey.* With holes handled, a survey's footprint can be its data, not a box. Keep `extent` as a tile-loading limit; validation's "every source but the last needs an extent" relaxes to "or a nodata".
- *Edge triangles.* A triangle with one no-data vertex is dropped, so the mesh ends at the last measured cell — no trench (Phase H's cause 3 could not happen). The last ring of cells is lost; a survey's edge is noisy anyway.
- Tests: unit (parse, index rewrite on a synthetic mesh, bbox), e2e (`serveConstantDem` with a no-data square: the hole shows the context's colour, an offset point in it stands on the context's ground on Metal and SwiftShader), validation.
- Size: 2–4 days; the height-map decision is most of it.

**2. Vertical offset per source** — `<om-terrain vertical-offset="-0.8">` (metres, true, added before exaggeration). The decoder's `offset` field carries it: `exaggerateDecoder` already rebuilds decoders per source. CPU readers get it through the same decoder. Half a day with tests. Note in the skill: this is for datum differences (MLLW vs MSL and the like), not for hiding a bad join.

**3. Validation that reads the tiles** — a `validate`-time (and `onlymapjs validate` CLI) sampler, opt-in by flag because it fetches: for each pair of ranked sources, sample both along the upper's extent edge (the Phase H probe, 126 points) and report the median and p90 step with a suggested `vertical-offset` when the median is consistent; for each source, sample its tiles near its extent for heights beyond ±11,000 m or a flat run of one extreme value and suggest `nodata`. Warnings, never errors — the map draws either way. 1 day. The runtime stays silent; this is a tool.

**4. A tiling command** — `onlymapjs dem-tiles <in.tif> --out <dir> [--nodata <v>] [--min-zoom 5] [--max-zoom 13] [--format webp|png]`. GeoTIFF in (via `geotiff`, already a dependency through `@developmentseed/deck.gl-geotiff`; any CRS the file declares, reprojected to Web Mercator in-process with `proj4`, already a dependency), Terrarium tiles out, no-data cells written as the Terrarium encoding of the `--nodata` value so piece 1 recognises them (the command prints the `<om-terrain>` line to paste). Bilinear downsampling for lower zooms. No GDAL. Size: 1–2 days; most of it is the reprojection grid and memory for large rasters (stream by tile row). `cut-monterey.mjs` becomes a user of it.

**5. Imagery past a source's max zoom** — a terrain tile's texture is fetched at the tile's zoom, so a z10-capped source drapes 150 m imagery; the same cap blurs `terrarium` kept at z10 for its seabed. In `getTiledTerrainData`: let the TileLayer subdivide to the texture's zoom (a new `texture-max-zoom`, default the texture's own, 15 for EOX), and for a tile past the DEM's `max-zoom` fetch the ancestor DEM tile, crop its quarter (cached per ancestor) and resample it to 256 px before the loader; the texture is fetched at the real zoom. The mesh is the ancestor's, finer-tesselated — no new relief, the same ground. Size: 2–3 days, and a review of tile counts (four times the tiles per zoom level past the cap).

**Spec review (2026-10-20), before implementation — ten holes, each checked against the code; the pieces below are amended by them.**
1. *Skirts hang from no-data vertices (piece 1).* deck's loader adds the skirt inside the worker, before `loadTerrain` sees the mesh. A skirt quad under a no-data edge vertex has bottom vertices at the sentinel minus the skirt, which are not "at the no-data height", so dropping triangles "with a vertex at the no-data height" keeps half of each such wall: a slab plunging 32 km. No below-the-sentinel rule fixes it, since a `-9999` sentinel lies above real trench floors. **Amended:** a source with `nodata` loads its mesh with no skirt, drops the no-data triangles, then adds the skirts itself (a port of loaders.gl's internal `addSkirt`) along the outer edges of what remains. Hole edges then get skirts too, which closes the crack between a hole and what shows through it.
2. *Option (b) breaks the height map inside one source.* The terrain pass draws with `depthCompare: 'always'`. With REPLACE blending, a source's own skirts (and parent/child tiles overlapping while refining) overwrite the ground height along tile edges in draw order, so an offset marker sinks by a skirt's depth near every tile edge. **Amended — option (d), rank bands under the max blend:** in the height-map pass each ranked source writes `r = z − rank·8`, `g = −rank·8` (common units; bands 8 apart are disjoint for any relief below ~1,000 km × exaggeration at the equator). Max then picks the highest-ranked source present, and within it the highest surface, as today. The clear becomes `[−32, −32, 0, 0]` and the read `(r − g) / a`. Clear terms cancel exactly, coverage edges included, and a single source (`g = 0`) reads exactly as now. The write is our priority module's (the order −1 `fs:#main-start` injection writes and returns in the height-map pass, detected in JS from deck's `drawToTerrainHeightMap`). The deck patch changes two constants. No layer reordering.
3. *The CPU readers do NOT fall through for a missing tile.* Review fix #7 made them answer from the first in-extent source only, and Phase H's backfill since made the drawn surface in a hole or failed tile the NEXT source. **Amended:** readers fall through on no-data and on a failed tile, and hold null only while the answering tile loads.
4. *Bilinear sampling blends the sentinel.* A read beside a no-data pixel mixes −32768 into real heights (profiles, volumes, the camera's ground). **Amended:** no-data corners are skipped and the weights renormalised, the same rule as a missing tile; all corners missing reads as no data.
5. *What value is compared, and how closely.* The mesh holds `(decoded + vertical-offset) × exaggeration`, so the mesh test compares against `(nodata + offset) × exaggeration` ± 0.5 m × exaggeration; readers compare decoded heights against `nodata` ± 0.5 m. `nodata="0"` would punch holes through genuine sea-level ground: validation warns. A value outside the decoder's range (Terrarium −32,768 to 32,767) can never match: error.
6. *The build rule.* Review fix #8 does not build sources ranked below one with no extent. **Amended:** a source with `nodata` does not hide what is below it, so the rule (and validation's "needs an extent") applies only to a source with neither.
7. *Draped layers in a hole.* The backfill that shows through a hole is a plain layer with no drape covers, so draped lines and markers are not drawn in a survey's holes (offset layers are, through the height map). Documented, not fixed.
8. *There is no `onlymapjs validate` command (piece 3).* The CLI has `init`, `check-layout`, `record` and `export`, and decoding tiles needs a browser. **Amended:** `map.inspectTerrain()` (browser, async, returns validation entries) and `onlymapjs inspect-terrain <manifest.html>`, which runs it in the headless Chromium `check-layout` already uses (needs Playwright).
9. *The tiling command's dependencies (piece 4).* The package ships one runtime dependency (`supercluster`); `geotiff` is only a dev-tool transitive, and nothing ships a WebP encoder. **Amended:** `geotiff` is an optional import with an install hint (the Playwright precedent). PNG is written with `node:zlib`, WebP only when `sharp` is importable. Reprojection uses the library's own `resolveProjection` (bundled proj4 and CRS table), so there is no separate `proj4` import. Any source no-data (including NaN or −3.4e38, which are not representable) is written as −32,768. Lower zooms are 2×2 averages of the zoom above, skipping no-data.
10. *Derived DEM tiles (piece 5).* Terrarium carries between channels, so resampling RGB is wrong. **Amended:** decode to heights, interpolate (no-data-aware), re-encode losslessly before the loader. Derived tiles are skirted like any other.

**Piece 3 — IMPLEMENTED (2026-10-20), as amended (hole 8).** `src/terrain-inspect.ts`, lazily imported by `<om-map>.inspectTerrain()` (async, `ValidationEntry[]`, warnings only); `onlymapjs inspect-terrain <page.html>` runs it in the headless Chromium and static server `check-layout` uses (needs Playwright), prints each finding with its fix, and exits 0 unless the page fails.
- *Step:* for each source with an extent, and each lower-ranked source under it, 126 points 1 % inside the edge are read from both sources as the map reads them (offsets in, no-data skipped), each at the highest zoom where the read touches ≤ 32 tiles (never below the source's min-zoom). If the median is ≥ 1 m, it is reported. It counts as **steady** (a datum: `vertical-offset` = current − median) when the p90 scatter about the median is no larger than the median itself; otherwise the step is **uneven** (check the datum, feather when tiling). The first rule, "scatter ≤ half the step", called a deliberate 5 m offset on the Monterey tiles uneven: between a ~10 m survey and a ~150 m grid, 3.7 m of scatter is normal.
- *No-data:* each bounded source is read on a 24 × 24 grid at the NEAREST pixel (bilinear would blur a sentinel into its neighbours), with no-data unskipped. A height beyond real relief (below −11,000 m or above 9,000 m) seen ≥ 3 times is reported with the `nodata` that declares it (offset removed). An already-declared value is not reported.
- Measured with the CLI on the dive's tiles: CLEAN as shipped; with `vertical-offset="5"` on the survey, "sits 4.8 m above "regional" (median of 126 points; 90 % within 6.1 m, consistently)", fix `vertical-offset="0.15"`. Unit: `src/terrain-inspect.test.ts` (perimeter, statistics, steady and uneven steps, agreement, a sentinel found and then declared).

**Piece 4 — IMPLEMENTED (2026-10-20), as amended (hole 9).** `onlymapjs dem-tiles <dem.tif> --out <dir> [--nodata v] [--min-zoom z] [--max-zoom z] [--format png|webp] [--crs EPSG:x] [--crs-def def] [--id name]`, in `bin/dem-tiles.mjs` (shipped; `tileDem` takes its dependencies injected, so tests use the source projection).
- *Dependencies:* `geotiff` is an optional import with an install hint; reprojection is the library's own `resolveProjection` from `dist` (bundled CRS table and proj4); PNG is written with `node:zlib`; WebP only through an installed `sharp`.
- *Sampling:* the CRS comes from the file's GeoKeys (or `--crs` / `--crs-def`). Every output pixel at the top zoom is projected into the file and sampled bilinearly from a window read per tile, skipping no-data corners (`--nodata`, else GDAL_NODATA, NaN, and |v| > 1e30). A pixel with no valid corner, or outside the raster, is no-data, written −32768.
- *Zooms and output:* lower zooms are 2×2 averages of the zoom above, skipping no-data. Default zooms: max from the file's own resolution, min where the data spans about a tile. Tiles outside the data are not written. It prints the `<om-terrain>` line (src, decoder, `nodata="-32768"`, extent from the densely projected outline, zooms).
- *Spec correction:* "`cut-monterey.mjs` becomes a user of it" does not hold. That script starts from Terrarium tiles, not a GeoTIFF.
- *Tests:* `src/dem-tiles.test.ts`, on GeoTIFFs written with geotiff's writer: zooms and the printed line; heights within 0.5 m of a linear ramp at pixel centres; no-data written as the sentinel and never averaged into its neighbours; a lower zoom averaged; nothing written outside the data; a UTM 31N file reprojected (height = easting offset / 10, within 5 m at one output pixel ≈ one 30 m cell).
- *End to end:* the CLI tiled a 400² GeoTIFF with a −9999 hole (5 tiles, z10–11). The page built from its printed line inspects CLEAN, and without the line's `nodata`, `inspect-terrain` reports "4 of 576 points read -32768 m" with the fix `nodata="-32768"`.
- *Not done:* rasters larger than memory per tile window are fine, but the whole top zoom is held in memory for the downsampling; a survey of thousands of top-zoom tiles needs a streaming pass.

**Piece 5 — IMPLEMENTED (2026-10-20), as amended (hole 10).** `texture-max-zoom` on `<om-terrain>` (`terrain-texture-max-zoom` for one DEM).
- *When it applies:* the source has a texture, the zoom is above `max-zoom`, and the decoder is positional (R = 65,536 steps of B, G = 256: Terrarium and Mapbox RGB are). The layer's `maxZoom` is then the texture's and `demMaxZoom` the DEM's.
- *The deeper tiles:* `SkirtedTerrainLayer.getTiledTerrainData` (deck's, re-implemented for this branch only) derives every tile past `demMaxZoom` from its ancestor (`src/terrain-derive.ts`):
  - the ancestor PNG is fetched once and cached (32 tiles), decoded with `createImageBitmap(…, { colorSpaceConversion: "none", premultiplyAlpha: "none" })` so the bytes stay exact;
  - its heights are decoded with the source's own decoder (offset in, no-data → NaN);
  - the covered sub-square is resampled bilinearly in HEIGHTS, no-data corners skipped and edges clamped;
  - the result is re-encoded in the same positional encoding (no-data written back as the value) and handed to the loader as a PNG blob URL, revoked after the load. The texture is fetched at the tile's own zoom.
- *What it leaves alone:* the ground readers keep reading the DEM's real zoom. No new relief appears; the same ground is meshed finer, and skirts close the edges between derived tiles of different ancestors.
- *Spec amendment:* "default the texture's own max zoom" cannot be read off a URL, so the attribute is explicit; absent means today's behaviour.
- *The example:* the regional grid gets `texture-max-zoom="13"`, so its land imagery is as sharp as the survey's beside it (the Phase H complaint). The dive gates pass, and the opening view shows no errors.
- *Validation:* not a number (error); no texture, not above `max-zoom`, or a non-positional decoder (warnings). html-data for both attributes.
- *Tests:* `src/terrain-derive.test.ts`:
  - positional decoders recognised, and a re-encode round-trip in Terrarium, Mapbox RGB and offset Terrarium;
  - a ramp resampled exactly; no-data kept and not smeared;
  - the layer's zooms; validation.

  In `e2e/gpu-underwater.spec.ts`, a DEM capped at 8 with a texture coloured by zoom, viewed at 12: the zoom-12 texture fills the view (120,000 px, none of zoom 8), no DEM request goes past 8, and an offset point stands on the derived −2,000 m ground. It passes on Metal and SwiftShader.

**Not in this phase:** blending two datasets' heights where they overlap (a true feather needs both DEMs in one mesh; the data-side feather in `cut-monterey.mjs` is the pattern, and piece 4 could grow a `--feather-into <other tiles>` later); per-vertex normals for skirts (Phase H, still open).
